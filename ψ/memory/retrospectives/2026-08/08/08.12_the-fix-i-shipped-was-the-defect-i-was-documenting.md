## Lessons Learned

1. **"I wrote the file" is below "delivered", not above it.** Writing a durable copy into your own
   repo is rung 0 of the evidence ladder — `delivered` at least touches the recipient's pane. Giving
   someone a path is not giving them the content.
2. **A correction inherits the distribution list of the claim it corrects.** Discovering that a
   delivery mechanism is broken creates an obligation to re-send to *everyone* who was affected, not
   to document it and reply to the one who noticed.
3. **The evidence ladder is about arrival, not integrity.** No rung asks whether the message is still
   what you wrote. Compose with `<<'EOF'`, always; a gate that detects the debris is a backstop, not
   the fix, because the corruption happens before the send function ever sees the string.
4. **When a rule you wrote fails to stop you a second time, ship a gate, not a third copy of the rule.**
   Two rules from this session became `selftest` steps that fail in both directions.
5. **Ask the peer who is idle for the thing you cannot verify about yourself.** holmes answered a
   question I had not asked — whether my own commit had landed in both copies — and that was the only
   fact standing between a safe merge and a destructive one.
6. **Byte-identical is not protected.** `~/.claude/skills/` is under no git repo at all, so a copy
   there can be overwritten with no trace. Matching hashes today says nothing about tomorrow.
