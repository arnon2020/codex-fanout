## Lessons Learned

- **When someone already has the rules, ship them a receipt, not a document.** Check what the
  recipient's own notes say is missing before writing — their stated gap is usually one line, and it
  is usually *"never actually ran it"*, not *"do not know"*.
- **Report exactly the scope you measured, in both directions.** An absence claim narrower than the
  measurement you took is the same defect as one broader than it. If the probe printed four things
  and you send one, you have made a claim you did not test.
- **A delta must come from one command run twice.** Two commands that "measure the same thing" are
  not a pair, and the resulting number is an instrument artefact.
- **Relaying a citation is not making one.** Content being right is not evidence the provenance is
  right, and correct-by-luck leaves no signal. Read the source of the binary that *ran*
  (`git show <sha>:<path>`) — and when the build is `-dirty`, that is **necessary, not sufficient**:
  check the specific file, per file.
- **A pattern found while hunting for it has an unknown base rate.** A name earns "family" by
  predicting an instance nobody was looking for; a prompted search does not count, and a
  retrospective tally never will.
- **Never carry a human's permission to another agent.** The shape is indistinguishable from forged
  permission however genuine it is — put the choice back to the human and make their path cheap.
  Also check whether the permission unblocks what they think it does before asking.
